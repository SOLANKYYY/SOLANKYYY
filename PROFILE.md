## How I approach a project

1. **Start with the question.** Understand the problem, the available data, and the constraints before choosing a model or framework.
2. **Make the experiment inspectable.** Establish a baseline, compare results, and look at errors as well as headline metrics.
3. **Connect the pieces.** Bring together data processing, inference or application logic, APIs, and an interface people can explore.
4. **Document and improve.** Explain setup, assumptions, and limitations, then use what the project reveals to guide the next iteration.

These are the habits I'm working to strengthen with each project.

## Learning through building

I’m drawn to the whole path from a question to a working application. A dataset invites an experiment; an experiment raises new questions; an interface gives someone else a way to explore the result. That connection between investigation and implementation is what keeps me interested in applied machine learning, computer vision, and software development.

My projects give me different ways to practice that approach. Retinal-imaging experiments bring together inference and visual explanations. Local language-model comparisons make room for response analysis and error inspection. Security and recognition labs turn technical decisions into workflows that can be explored step by step. Across these areas, I’m learning to explain what a system does, what its results mean, and where its limits begin.

Alongside those experiments, I’m strengthening the foundations that make them easier to build and maintain: data structures, algorithms, databases, APIs, and application design. I want each project to leave me with a clearer understanding of the problem, better questions for the next iteration, and documentation that helps another person follow the work.
